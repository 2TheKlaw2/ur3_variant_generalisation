# Capstone evidence brief: DMP adaptation, variant assembly and UR3e force control

Prepared 31 October 2026 for Enrique’s UR3e/RG2 capstone. I treat the 31 October handover and its addendum as the current project record; project results below are supplied results, not independently rerun experiments. This is a research brief and proposed evaluation framework, not approval to execute physical motion.

## Decision summary

- **Fix B has a defensible literature basis:** additive coupling of a DMP’s transformation dynamics is established, and potential-field coupling has been used to deform learned paths around obstacles; this supports the method class, not a guarantee that a height ceiling avoids UR3e singularities ([Ijspeert et al., 2013](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/7874487/NECO_a_00393.pdf); [Park et al., 2008](https://www.heikohoffmann.de/documents/park_humanoids08.pdf)). I would call the proposed implementation “a workspace-height coupling heuristic intended to improve kinematic feasibility.”
- **Joint limits need an explicit verification gate:** UR’s freshly fetched PolyScope 5.24 manual says elbow ±160°, whereas its freshly fetched October 2026 datasheet says elbow ±360°; both agree on maximum speeds of 180°/s for base/shoulder/elbow and 360°/s for the wrists ([UR manual](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_24/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR3e/H_g5_sections/appendix_g5/tech_spec_data.htm); [UR datasheet](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/DataSheets/UR3e_techsheet_pdf_online/UR3e_techsheet_en.pdf)). I would not approve either candidate posture using an assumed ±π elbow bound.
- **Retain the CAD-only baseline:** relevant papers compare learned policies, constraint ablations, or model-based planners, but the selected LfD papers do not establish superiority over a matched CAD-only assembly baseline ([C-LEARN](https://ai.stanford.edu/~cdarpino/CLEARN/ICRA17_DArpino_CLEARN.pdf); [Davchev et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.07682); [Tian et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yunsheng/Assemble-Them-All/paper.pdf)). The supplied L2 = CAD result, 5/5 in rigid kinematic simulation, should remain visible.
- **There is no transferable “safe collaborative speed”:** UR makes application risk assessment the basis for safety settings, while its 250 mm/s instruction is specifically scoped to manual hand-guiding with linear movements unless a risk assessment permits more ([UR3e manual, pp. 23–24 and 32](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW10_6/user-manual-UR3e-PolyX-PDF_online/708-825-00_UR3e%20PolyScope%20X_User_Manual_PolyScopeX_en_Global.pdf)). The project’s 0.14 rad/s pilot guard is therefore a project setting, not a manufacturer-certified safe speed.
- **Published force examples are useful but not plug-in settings:** one UR3e insertion study used desired forces of 5–10 N with a custom compliance controller; a UR5e native-force-mode study used 1–10 N against surfaces, including speed limits of 1 and 10 mm/s in separate experiments ([Beltran-Hernandez et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.10224v3); [Trochimczuk et al., 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/5/1709)). I did not verify a directly transferable UR e-series bayonet torque/angular-speed prescription.

### What fix B actually changes

The standard additive form is

\[
\tau\dot v=\alpha\bigl(\beta(g-x)-v\bigr)+f(s)+C(x,v,s),\qquad
\tau\dot x=v.
\]

Here \(f(s)\) is the learned forcing term and \(C\) is an added coupling input; keeping \(f\) fixed does not keep the resulting path fixed ([Ijspeert et al., Eq. 3.1](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/7874487/NECO_a_00393.pdf)). I would describe B as a demo-conditioned but deliberately modified trajectory, not replay of the same demonstrated path.

As an illustrative design, not an implementation claim, one could define a downward response \(C_z=-k\max(0,z-z_c)\), smoothed at activation and supplemented with appropriately gated damping. I would first inspect how Victor’s `ext_force` enters the integration equations: the input’s units, coordinate frame and dependence on \(\tau\) must be established before treating \(k\) as a physical or transferable gain.

My interpretation for this project is that lowering the transport arc may avoid the problematic extended-elbow region, but a ceiling does not directly constrain the Jacobian or joint angles. I would not call the method “singularity avoidance” without logging the actual joint-space margin and demonstrating that the claimed singularity is avoided.

The fixed-base, six-joint UR3e provides six rotating joints according to UR; the mobile-coupling paper obtains its additional freedom from a nine-DoF arm-plus-base system ([UR specifications](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_24/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR3e/H_g5_sections/appendix_g5/tech_spec_data.htm); [Tu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.03296v2)). As a kinematic deduction, a nonsingular square 6×6 Jacobian has no local null space for a fully specified six-dimensional TCP task, so I would not transplant a redundant-arm avoidance formula without relaxing a task constraint or changing the branch/path.

### Proposed evaluation of A versus B

These are my proposed checks, not completed experiments. I would use the same geometry and registration for all conditions and retain every failed run.

- **Baseline:** preserve the unmodified \(\tau=2\) V4/V5 trajectories and recorded failure locations.
- **Posture A:** check candidate joint limits and cable constraints, then validate the entire HOME reconfiguration and full sequence with whole-arm collision checking.
- **Coupling B:** record ceiling, gains, activation/smoothing, integration step, timing and the unchanged learned-weight identifier. Apply a common selection rule across variants where feasible, and disclose any per-variant tuning.
- **Kinematic margins:** log all joint positions, minimum distance to configured position limits, joint speed/acceleration, and a Jacobian singularity measure along the continuous branch. Define the translational/rotational scaling before comparing singular values or condition numbers.
- **Task preservation:** measure deviation from the uncoupled trajectory, fixture clearance, start/end continuity and Stage 9 handover error. Preserve the actual CAD and report any new collisions rather than editing the geometry.
- **Selection discipline:** choose feasibility and safety gates before examining which setting gives the best assembly result. Count manually selected ceilings or postures as engineering intervention even when they require no new demonstration.

Suggested report wording:

> I introduced a workspace-height coupling term into the Cartesian DMP transformation dynamics while retaining the forcing weights learned from the V1 demonstration, following the established additive-coupling approach ([Ijspeert et al., 2013](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/7874487/NECO_a_00393.pdf); [Park et al., 2008](https://www.heikohoffmann.de/documents/park_humanoids08.pdf)). I evaluated whether this heuristic improved continuous inverse-kinematics feasibility for V4 and V5; it was not assumed to guarantee singularity avoidance.

Use that wording only after implementing and evaluating B. Until then, change “introduced/evaluated” to “propose/will evaluate.”

### A fair comparison for the report

The handover reports L0 1/5, L1 3/5, L2 5/5 and CAD-only 5/5 for Stage 9 kinematic insertion/rotation. Those are coverage counts across five designed variants, not repeated physical-trial success estimates, and they do not establish a demonstration advantage.

I recommend retaining the following experimental distinctions:

- **Geometry adaptation:** compare L0, L1 and L2 to show where replay and simple goal scaling cease to cover the variant set.
- **Demonstration contribution:** compare L2 against CAD-only with identical meshes, registration, controller, IK acceptance criteria, collision checking and endpoint tolerances.
- **Timing fairness:** report both the current native-timing comparison and, if possible, a matched-duration/matched-speed-limit comparison. Otherwise timing and trajectory-shape effects remain mixed.
- **Force-control fairness:** if compliance is added, apply the same compliance settings to both L2 and CAD-only, or label controller choice as a separate experimental factor.
- **Insertion versus full sequence:** keep Stage 9 and Stage 10 result tables separate. A whole-sequence comparison needs a defined non-demo REACH/TRANSPORT baseline; reusing demo-derived transport for the “CAD-only” condition would not make that whole sequence demonstration-free.
- **Physical evidence:** report successful locked assemblies out of attempts per variant, seating gap/yaw, measured axial and lateral forces, axial torque, retries, cycle time, protective stops and damage observations. Do not infer all of these from commanded values.
- **Robustness:** predeclare plate translation/rotation perturbations and repeated-trial counts rather than testing only conditions found to work. Report engineering time and per-variant manual interventions alongside performance.

Suggested interpretation:

> In the supplied rigid kinematic insertion tests, geometry-conditioned LfD and the CAD-only baseline both assembled all five variants. This shows that the geometry-conditioned path covered the tested family, but does not show that the demonstration improved assembly success over CAD-only planning; physical robustness, contact behaviour or reduced engineering effort remain hypotheses to test.

### Collaborative speeds: quote the scope, not just the number

UR states: “If the robot is used in manual hand-guiding applications with linear movements, the speed limit must be set to maximum 250 mm/s for the tool/end effector and elbow unless a risk assessment shows that higher speeds are acceptable” ([UR3e PolyScope X user manual, p. 32](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW10_6/user-manual-UR3e-PolyX-PDF_online/708-825-00_UR3e%20PolyScope%20X_User_Manual_PolyScopeX_en_Global.pdf)). This is not a blanket assertion that 250 mm/s is safe for automatic insertion beside a fixture.

The same manual states: “The application risk assessment is the responsibility of the integrator,” and requires consideration of reach, motion, payload and speed of the robot, end effector and workpiece ([pp. 23–24](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW10_6/user-manual-UR3e-PolyX-PDF_online/708-825-00_UR3e%20PolyScope%20X_User_Manual_PolyScopeX_en_Global.pdf)). Particularly relevant here, UR warns: “As the robot stretches out, the knee-joint effect can give high forces in the radial direction (away from the base) at low speeds” ([p. 32](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/PDF/SW10_6/user-manual-UR3e-PolyX-PDF_online/708-825-00_UR3e%20PolyScope%20X_User_Manual_PolyScopeX_en_Global.pdf)).

I would keep the supplied 0.14 rad/s physical-pilot guard as a project constraint pending the lab’s review, and explicitly separate it from UR’s hardware maximums. A joint-speed cap alone does not specify TCP/elbow linear speed, and slowing the demonstrated path does not resolve the reported branch failure.

### Official force-mode semantics and warnings

- **Selection and frame:** `selection_vector` marks compliant axes, and `type=2` leaves the task frame untransformed; wrench entries on non-compliant axes have no effect ([URScript reference](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_20/Content/prod-scriptmanual/G5/force_mode_task_frame_selection_vector.htm)).
- **Limits are not force thresholds:** UR says, “For compliant axes, these values are the maximum allowed tcp speed along/about the axis,” while for non-compliant axes they limit deviation from the programmed pose ([URScript reference](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_20/Content/prod-scriptmanual/G5/force_mode_task_frame_selection_vector.htm)).
- **Transitions matter:** UR advises avoiding motion parallel to compliant axes and high deceleration immediately before entry, considering a pause of at least 0.02 s, and avoiding high acceleration in force mode because it reduces accuracy ([URScript reference](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_20/Content/prod-scriptmanual/G5/force_mode_task_frame_selection_vector.htm)).
- **Gain is not a harmless tuning knob:** `force_mode_set_gain_scaling` defaults to 1 and UR warns that a value above 1 can cause instability against hard surfaces or during collisions ([UR gain documentation](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_20/Content/prod-scriptmanual/G5/force_mode_set_gain_scaling.htm)). I would not copy the 2026 surface-study gain recommendations, which include values above 1 and differ between its table and narrative, into the bayonet test ([surface study](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/5/1709)).
- **Low-force interpretation needs care:** the October 2026 UR3e datasheet gives force-sensor accuracy ±3.5 N and precision ±2.0 N, with torque accuracy/precision ±0.1 Nm ([UR datasheet](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/DataSheets/UR3e_techsheet_pdf_online/UR3e_techsheet_en.pdf)). Those specifications do not prohibit low-force tests, but I would not claim sub-newton physical improvements from an unvalidated built-in-sensor trace.

### Proposed staged study, not an approved execution recipe

I would keep force mode separate from the core kinematic study and require the lab’s operator approval at each physical stage. No new motion script or controller change is authorised by this brief.

- **Before contact:** verify the real registration, TCP, payload, grasp retention, task-frame directions, stop behaviour and controller/ROS ownership. Establish the zero-load force/torque baseline and a way to distinguish sensor bias, fixture loading and actual part contact.
- **Straight insertion first:** evaluate V1 separately before adding rotation or V5’s helical motion. Select and approve the force target, compliant-axis speed, travel envelope, timeout and abort thresholds using the actual printed parts and fixture, rather than a paper’s setpoint.
- **Keep commands and limits distinct:** log commanded wrench, measured wrench and abort thresholds separately. Treat software monitoring as experimental supervision, not a replacement for configured safety functions or safeguarding.
- **Locking phase:** define the insertion/rotation transition from observed seat position and contact state, not elapsed time alone. For V5, preserve its CAD-derived axial/yaw relationship rather than imposing a fixed-z rotation simply because a simpler bayonet permits it.
- **Failure handling:** stop on exceeded approved force/torque, unexpected pose error, lack of progress, gripper slip or a protective stop; require review rather than an automatic increase in pushing force.
- **Evidence quality:** record force/torque time histories, pose, joint states, phase, operator confirmations, termination reason and post-test inspection. If force differences are central to the thesis, establish measurement uncertainty or obtain an independently validated sensor.
